# Stashtronauts

A friendly money tracker where savings goals are planets in a calm 3D space scene, and you fly between them in a small ship that is also your pilot's home.

## Platform: desktop first
- Design and test for a wide landscape browser window with a mouse and keyboard (check at about 1440 by 900).
- No on-screen joystick or touch controls for now. Do not design screens like a phone: no bottom sheets, slide-up tabs, or single-column stacks as the main layout.
- A phone layout is a separate, later task. Until then, narrow windows only get a plain fallback so nothing breaks (the `max-width:900px` block in `console.css`).

## Overall vision
- Static site: plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript ES modules, three.js from a CDN through the import map in `index.html`, no build step, `localStorage` for data.
- Look and feel: chunky, colorful, stylized, calm and toy-like, inspired by the feel of Astroneer. Never copy anyone's characters or assets.
- The pilot is a human-proportioned person (about six heads tall), stylized low-poly, not round or bubbly. This replaces the earlier "round Tomodachi style" idea for the pilot.
- Words: plain English, warm, never shaming. Explain any finance term in friendly words.
- Motion: respect `prefers-reduced-motion` (no autopilot, no walking: the pilot stays seated). Planets rotate slowly. When nobody is steering, the ship cruises on autopilot between random waypoints and hovers between trips (this reverses the earlier idea that the ship only moves when steered). Other ships and objects appear only when something is happening.

## Build order (build one phase at a time, then stop for feedback)
- **Phase A (built):** the player's ship and pilot: a big, old, much-modified two-story ship that is the pilot's home.
  - Hull (`hull.js`): an angular body lofted through cross-sections, not a tube. Pointed prow under a cockpit canopy with a wraparound windshield, a wide flat lower hull with a chine, a stepped and raised back section, collars between sections, armour plates.
  - Outside colours: rugged and rustic, not the old cream/teal/orange. Weathered khaki and olive panels, rust brown, charcoal trim and a few burnt-orange accents, with heavy wear drawn in code (`loftTextures`, `wornTexture` in `textures.js`): faded and peeling paint, rust streaks and patches, scratches, oil and grime runs, scorch near the engines, mismatched replacement panels. The HUD, console and interior keep the cozy cream/teal/orange palette.
  - Bolted on (`exterior.js`): dorsal spine with pipes, stub wings with two thruster nacelles hanging on pylons under them (with clear space between wing and nacelle), cargo pods on the left flank and an added-on module on the right (deliberately asymmetric), observation dome, radiator fins, folded solar wings, mismatched antennas and dishes, a cargo door, a plated belly with hatches, sensor pods and ventral fins. No tail fin on the roof. Nothing is drawn around the hull. `ship.checkExterior()` lists bounding-box overlaps between the bolted-on parts and how deep each sinks into the hull (the remaining overlaps are bounding-box only: the big dish above the spine, and the parallel radiator fins).
  - Landing gear: four big legs (hydraulic cylinder and piston, diagonal brace, bolted joints, wide pads) folded away in belly wells by default, never out in flight or while hovering. Landing (Phase E) will call `ship.setLandingGear(true/false)`. Until then the L key is a temporary debug toggle (marked DEBUG in `space.js`).
  - Interior (`interior.js`, meters; rooms 6.8 m wide). UPPER DECK: the bridge (a wide curved console packed with switches, buttons, sliders, dials and keypads; seven animated screens: camera feed, engine, navigation, budget, radar, systems, power; overhead panel; pilot and co-pilot side consoles; comms station; nav table with a star map and hologram; racks with lights; lockers; labels). LOWER DECK, front to back: living quarters (bunk room, bathroom, galley with the pantry, lounge with the desk), science lab (bench, microscope, centrifuge, plant jars, specimen tanks, whiteboard, hydroponics rack, hazard cabinet, sample freezer), suit and workshop room (three suits on hooks with helmets above and boots below, O2 packs, tether reels, a bench, lockers with maintenance gear, the mechanic's workbench, pegboard, 3D printer, labeled bins), airlock, storage room (floor-to-ceiling racks of labeled boxes and crates, the junk and overflow). Behind the bridge the lower rooms are double height, with wall cabinets, shelves and a duct up high. BOTTOM DECK: fuel and data only (fuel tanks, pipes, valves, gauges, pumps, coolant exchanger, glowing coolant line, a catwalk, rows of server racks with status lights, engine access hatch).
  - Floors stay clear: every item sits on a shelf, hook, rack, table, in a crate or a locker. At most one rug per room. `ship.blockedPaths` lists anything standing on a walking path (only the destination furniture is expected: seat, bed, sofa, chairs, the shower tray).
  - Ladders: bridge to lower deck, and lower deck to bottom deck, each through a hatch with railings. The crew turns to face the ladder, steps on, climbs one rung at a time (hands and feet alternating, body shifting, head following the hands), and steps off; going down also faces the ladder. Same code for every ladder (`layout.ladders` in `interior.js`, climbing in `crew.js`, the pose in `pilot.js`).
  - Money shows inside only: savings jar, a progress frame per goal on the lounge wall, trophies for finished goals, and the pantry, whose fullness is the emergency fund. Optional debt tow pod stays. Outside, the emergency fund is the guardian fleet (below).
  - Camera: one state machine in `space.js`: outside -> entering -> inside -> leaving, plus free. "Look closer" (or scrolling in past 1x the ship length) enters; "Back outside", scrolling out past 1.8x, or any flight key leaves; scrolling during a glide is ignored; nothing else opens or closes the hull. Inside, the ship holds still, you drag to orbit and scroll to zoom, and the camera stays outside the ship's outline. Free camera (button or F): the hull opens, the camera detaches (W/A/S/D move, Q/E down/up, drag to look, scroll sets speed, Shift faster), flight keys do nothing to the ship and the autopilot pauses; the cut stays on one side and only switches once the camera is well clear of the hull on the other side. Free camera button or Esc glides back to the follow camera and closes the hull.
  - Flying: W/A/S/D or arrows, Space up, C or Ctrl down, Shift boost (1.8x top speed); the ship pitches a little when climbing or sinking. Keys are ignored while the console or a text field is open; while Ctrl is held, Ctrl+key browser shortcuts are blocked where the browser allows (Ctrl+W/T/N can't be blocked by any page, so C is the recommended down key). A small controls hint shows on the first visit; the full list is in the console under "Ship and pilot".
  - The pilot lives aboard: seated at the controls while the ship moves, and doing activities while it hovers (cook then eat, nap, read, shower, garden, workbench, desk, star map, storage tidying, lab: experiment, microscope, whiteboard; and a ship check on the bottom deck: fuel gauges then the data racks). Activities are data in `crew.js`.
  - The console opens only from the Console button or a click on the bridge's budget screen; never by itself.
  - Autopilot cruising when idle outside; flight keys take over at once, and the autopilot returns a few seconds after you let go.
- **Emergency fund guardian fleet, Part 1 (built, `fleet.js`):** the emergency fund is not a planet and there is no shield: it's a calm, coast-guard-like fleet. Everything original (no film, game or real-military ships, fighters or insignia).
  - Mothership by months of costs saved (`shipMoney().emergencyMonths`): none without a fund (or at $0), patrol frigate under 3 months, cruiser 3 to 6, flagship carrier 6 or more. Sizes are multiples of the player's ship length (18.4): frigate 4x (74 units), cruiser 10.7x (197), carrier 32x (589, about the width of the largest planet). Small tiers get a minimum body size so the fighter-sized bay fits. Fighters keep their size. A tier change crossfades over about 3 s (a little muddy mid-way).
  - Silhouette (built per tier from a seed, slightly asymmetric, as if it grew over decades): a long pointed prow, a main deck of armoured segments with dark ribs and green bands, a keel, two stepped decks set back above it, long wing-like armour sections of different lengths on each side, a massive three-tier command tower plus smaller towers, sensor spires, a rear engine block with a grid of big engines (6 / 12 / 24) and two wing-tip thrusters, docking arms with clamp rings on the right and underneath, cargo gantries with containers, antenna forests (instanced), dish arrays, and on the cruiser and carrier a city of lit buildings (instanced) on the top deck, upper deck and wings. Paint unchanged: matte black and dark steel with dark green accents.
  - Readable at a distance: rim lighting and person-sized lit windows are added in the armour shader (`hullShader` in `fleet.js`, in ship space so they don't slide); whole decks are lit or dark, so far away they read as lines of light, and the pattern fades to a soft even glow instead of shimmering. Running lights along every edge, wing and tower (blue left, green right, red on spires), glowing engines, a pulsing beacon with slowly turning beams on the command tower, and a few fixed-size key lights that keep the outline readable from very far.
  - Rendering: the renderer uses a logarithmic depth buffer (far plane 40,000) so nothing flickers from the cabin to kilometres away; custom shaders (stars, atmospheres) include three's logdepth chunks, and any new custom shader must too. Level of detail: the fighter-sized bay interior is drawn only within 400 of the bay (a lit opening from further), markings and the bridge interior only when near, the bridge officers within 90.
  - Collision: the hull is a list of boxes in ship space (`D.boxes`). Fighters, the player's ship and the cameras are pushed out of their union (`pushOut`); fighters look ahead in proportion to their speed and detour under the keel when the hull is between them and where they're going. Fighters fly faster on bigger ships (x1 + L/800).
  - Station: on a shell outside 1.5x the planets' space (where the player's ship can never go) plus the hull's size, so it never overlaps a planet or traps the player. Fighters avoid the planets' air and patrol faster in the bigger world. Relocations (every 5 to 10 minutes) stay on that shell, turn at most about a radian, and speed up and slow down very slowly (top speed L/250, at least 6). On a tier change it moves to the new tier's shell. The free camera reaches it (range out to the fleet, scroll speed up to 900) and the fleet camera can fly alongside.
  - Bridge: a human-sized room with a sloped glass front on the command tower's face near the top, with officers at consoles.
  - Hangar bays (frigate 2, cruiser 8, carrier 30): side bays along both flanks, upper bays on the carrier's upper deck sides, underside bays beside the keel, and a rear flight deck facing aft on top of the engine block (plus the docking arms). Each bay is built once in its own frame (mouth at x = 0, out is -x, floor at y = 0, spots side by side along z) and placed with a turn about y (`tryBay` in `fleet.js`). A bay is only kept if its open mouth and its 48-unit launch lane are clear of every solid, and its block doesn't cut into another bay or the wings. The main bay (left flank) keeps the assembly bay, shutter and pad for new fighters. Each bay has its block, lit mouth, lip lights, guide beacons (blinking in turn, brighter while a fighter lands), and an inside (deck boxes, numbers, fuel and ammo racks, catwalk, stairs, gantries, refuel arms and hoses, crew door) drawn only within 260 of the bay; from further away a lit panel behind its back wall shows. Fighters return to a free spot near where they are, weighted against busy bays, so landings and launches come from all over the hull; several launch side by side.
  - Guns (frigate 6, cruiser 30, carrier 120; about a quarter heavy twin-barrel turrets, half point defence, a fifth missile batteries), instanced per kind as base, turning head and tilting guns, in the hull's palette. Mounts are picked on deck tops (upside down underneath), tower ledges, wings and the engine block; a gun's whole sweep (barrel reach all round) must be clear of solids, bays, lanes and other guns; the city, antennas and dishes keep clear of them. Idle: a slow sweep and scan with soft blinking sensor lights. Only updated when the camera is within 2.5 ship lengths + 800; far away the barrels are left out.
  - Firing (`stepCombat`): soft glowing tracer bolts and missiles with grey smoke, pooled (160 tracers, 24 missiles, smoke/spark/glow pools), soft hit sparks, no flashes. Turrets pick a target they can see (in range, above their own horizon), lead it, and fire only when lined up and `safeShot` passes: never within 60° of the player's ship or near it, never toward or near a planet, never through a fighter, never through the mothership's own hull.
  - Practice drills: every 2 to 4.5 minutes while the camera is near, 4 to 7 drones (tougher on bigger ships) drift in a training zone beyond the mothership on the side away from the player and the planets; turrets, missile batteries and one or two fighters engage them.
  - **Interception hook:** `fleet.intercept({ pos, vel, radius, hp, kind })` hands any threat (practice drones now, asteroids later) to the guns, missile batteries and fighters; bigger targets draw more turrets (`cap`) and more fighters. Fighters engaging hold their own stand-off point and fire only when safe.
  - Calm space (console "Controls and settings", `ship.calmSpace`, always on with reduced motion): guns hold still and fire nothing, no drills, fighters launch slowly.
  - Movement: holds station far from the player and every planet with a tiny drift. Every 3 to 7 minutes it relocates (a straight line, at least 0.6x the world size away, a turn of at most about 1 radian unless space is crowded) with a very slow speed-up and slow-down; it pauses if the player is ahead of it. `fleet.keepClear` keeps the player's ship and camera out of it, and the autopilot avoids it.
  - Fighters: original small jets (instanced parts) with a squadron stripe, a bubble canopy with a pilot (helmet colours vary; pilots drawn only within 70), twin engines with glow, skids, wing and landing lights. One per "Dollars per fighter" (setting in the console's "Controls and settings", saved as `ship.fighterUnit`, default $100). At most 24 fly; the rest are in reserve (spares are parked in empty bays). Far away a fighter becomes a simple stand-in, then a small teal navigation light.
  - Per fighter: patrol (fuel drains) -> return (to a bay on the ship wherever it is now; or hold off the mouth if no bay is free) -> approach (a curve onto the landing lights, straight in) -> turntable turn -> refuel (arm and hose, fuel fills; crews sometimes rotate with the reserve) -> ready -> board (a pilot walks from the crew door) -> launch out of the mouth. At least one or two stay docked. Patrol waypoints prefer grid cells nobody visited recently; separation steering plus a hard 6-unit minimum; occasional wingman pairs for a short leg; they keep clear of the player's ship, planets and the mothership. Distant fighters steer every 4th frame.
  - More money: one combined toast (e.g. "60 new fighters added"), and up to 40 new fighters roll out of the assembly bay one at a time (shutter opens, it rolls to the pad, turns, sparkles, the pilot waves, then launches) with a toast ("New fighter added"); if the wing is full the longest-docked fighter goes to the reserve. Less money: extras quietly retire to the reserve, no messages.
  - HUD: a shield button (hover, focus or click) with a plain-English line and "Show the fleet", which flies the camera to the mothership (`toFleet`/`fleet` camera modes: drag to circle, scroll to zoom, never inside the hull); "Back to ship", Esc, F or any flight key glide back. "Show in space" on the fund in the console does the same. The console calls the fund "Guardian fleet".
  - Reduced motion: the fleet runs at 15% speed, no relocating, no drift.
  - Done: many hangars and bays (frigate 2, cruiser 8, carrier about 30: a rear flight deck, side bays on both flanks, smaller bays top and bottom, docking arms; each with mouth, landing lights, beacons, interior, its own lanes; fighters use the bay nearest them; interiors only up close), and guns: heavy dual-barrel turrets, point-defence turrets and missile batteries (frigate about 6, cruiser about 30, carrier about 120) that track smoothly, sweep and scan when idle, and fire soft tracer bolts and missiles with smoke only at practice drones and later asteroids, always away from the player and planets; pooled projectiles, updated only when visible, still with reduced motion or Calm space.
  - Part 2 of the original fleet request (not built yet): weapons (tracer bullets, missiles), practice drills in far training zones, an interception hook for future asteroids and a hook for the player's ship to dock in the hangar, shooting stars, a "Calm space" console toggle (on with reduced motion), projectile pooling.

## Planets and atmospheres
- Size: a planet's full radius is 105 to 276 units (seeded from its name, same random sequence as before, remapped), and it starts at 70% and grows to 100% with its goal, so the smallest is at least 8 ship lengths across and the largest about 30. Debt planets (one per debt with a balance) use a dark storm biome; their storm is the balance compared with the most ever owed on that debt (`stashtronauts-debt-peaks`). With no saved goals, the examples include one debt planet ("Tempest").
- Spacing: planets sit far apart (`spreadFor` 2,600 + 1,500 x sqrt(slot + 1)), each keeping its reach (rings, moons and air) plus 1,200 clear of the others. Debt planets are placed after all goal planets.
- Detail: each surface has three levels (`THREE.LOD`, icosahedron detail 10 / 24 / 44; less on touch devices). The finest is built the first time the camera comes within 4.5 radii (about 90 ms once per planet). A seeded cloud shell (`cloudShell`) wraps each planet; the glow ring sits just outside it.
- Air (`atmosphere.js`): layers from the centre: highest ground, the cloud deck (at least a ship's size above it), the cloud layer (at least 35 thick), the upper layer (at least 45), and the outer glow zone. Approaching brightens the ring; the upper layer adds a faint wash of the planet's sky colour, dims the stars and flares the engine; the cloud layer adds moving cloud sheets, a soft shake and a faint shimmer (none with reduced motion). Debt planets: darker clouds, rain and soft lightning glows (never strobing), scaled by the storm. A small line names the layer (only while the camera follows the ship). Thicker air slows the ship.
- The old barrier was `keepClear()` pushing the ship out at 1.12x the radius; the ship now has `holdAtDeck()` instead: a soft spring at the bottom of the clouds with a hard floor well above the ground, and the line says "Surface coming soon". **Hook:** the descent to the surface (Phase E) plugs in at the marked `HOOK (Phase E)` in `holdAtDeck`, calling `descendToSurface(planet)`. Cameras stay above the highest ground; the autopilot and the fighters keep outside the upper layer (`autoClear`), and the autopilot climbs back out if it takes over inside.
- Flight: top speed 70; Shift + forward ramps up to 1,500 for crossing between planets (still limited to 20% until the pilot is seated). The autopilot flies long legs fast (up to 600) and slows near its waypoint. Dust fills a box around the camera so speed always reads.
- Rendering: logarithmic depth buffer, far plane 150,000, stars at 50,000 and the sun at 44,000 (both follow the camera). Floating origin: everything lives in `universe`; when the camera is 2,000 units from the render origin the universe is shifted so the camera is back at it. Positions in code stay in universe space; `camLookAt` converts for `lookAt`, and the ship's trail and tow pod (drawn in render space) are shifted by `ship.rebase`.
- Not built yet: training zones and shooting stars (fleet Part 2) will need to sit far from planets in this bigger space.

## How it's built
- `space.js`: the 3D world, camera and flight. Planets come from saved goals and are generated from their mythology name.
- `ship.js`: puts the ship together (materials, cutaway planes, engine, flame and trail, tow pod, money, landing-gear hook). `hull.js`: the hull's shape and where things sit on it. `exterior.js`: the hull mesh, windshield and everything outside, including the landing gear and the overlap check. `interior.js`: the decks and rooms, ladders, waypoints and spots. The interior and pilot are built in meters inside a group scaled by `S`.
- `pilot.js`: the pilot, built from swappable parts (head, 7 hair styles, face, outfit, accessory) with five moods and jointed poses. `crew.js`: waypoints, pathfinding and the activity list.
- `textures.js`: every texture, drawn on canvases at startup (there are no image files).
- `console.js` / `console.css`: the ship console, a tablet that opens over the world. Money is entered in the keypad dialog (`#pad`).
- `fleet.js`: the emergency fund's guardian fleet (mothership tiers, hangar, fighters and their state machine, people in the hangar and on the bridge). `space.js` passes it the fund (`setFund`), calls `update` each frame and owns the fleet camera and HUD.
- `atmosphere.js`: each planet's air profile and levels, and the effects of flying into it (tint, clouds, rain, lightning, shake, the layer line).
- `money.js`: saved data, plain-English money formatting, the payoff simulation (`sim()`), and the ship's money summary (`shipMoney()`), shared by everything.
- Parts talk through window events: `stash:change`, `stash:show`, `stash:thumb`, `stash:thumbs`, `stash:mood`, `stash:console` (the dashboard screen asks for the console).

## Rules worth keeping
- Saved data lives in `localStorage` under `stashtronauts-v1` (older data under `vaultcore-v3` is migrated). Never break existing saved data.
- Every field has a label, focus stays inside open dialogs, and flight keys are ignored while the console is open or you're typing.
- Keep it light for a laptop: low-poly, shared geometry and materials, instancing for repeated small things.
- Art direction is in `docs/STYLE.md`.
